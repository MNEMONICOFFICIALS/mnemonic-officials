transmission 001

about the broadcast

MNΞМΟNiC is a k-pop cover group broadcasting from an unknown location. No one knows who they are, where they transmit from, or how the recordings keep appearing — on old tapes, in static between radio stations, in the background of someone else's video. The music is always just familiar. The voices are almost recognizable. The signal cuts in and out. Some people think they're a glitch. Some think they're a warning. Some think if you listen closely enough, the broadcast is meant specifically for you. They never announce themselves. They just appear. And then they're gone.

transmission 003

the broadcast

the in-between

It doesn't happen on the hour or at the top of a playlist. It happens in the space between one song ending and another beginning, the three seconds of silence most people skip through. That's where MNΞМΟNiC lives — not before, not after, always in the gap.

the signal

Nobody uploads it. It just arrives — half-buried in a stranger's camera roll, looping on a burned CD nobody remembers burning, playing faintly from a radio that isn't plugged in. The signal doesn't ask permission to be heard.

the static

Before the sound, there's always the static — a held breath of white noise, like the broadcast is deciding whether you're ready. Some say the static is the point. That if you listen to it long enough, it starts to sound like your own name.

the remnants

Everyone who finds the broadcast leaves something behind, and everyone who finds the broadcast takes something with them. A lyric that won't leave. A color that means more than it should now. The remnants are proof that you were here, even after the signal moves on.

the heart

Underneath the static and the half-memories, there is something that isn't a trick of the signal at all — five people who chose each other before anyone was listening, and who would still be here even if no one ever finds the frequency again.
